Reference card

Print this card in a light, matte filament. Light blue or aqua works best because it stands out from both brass keys and the white glare on silver keys. Matte white is the next best choice. Avoid silk or glossy filament, grey, and yellow or gold.

The key lies flat in the recess for the side photos. For the keyway, print the profile gauge on the Keyway tab.

Printed the v2/v3 card with the slot? Keep the slot box ticked so the app knows where the recess is.

After printing: measure the card with calipers and type the real width and height above.

Using a real bank card instead? Set the recess depth to 0 and untick the slot. The slot-photo keyway method won't be available.

Taking good photos

  • Shoot straight down from about 30–40 cm. Use 2× zoom if your phone has it.
  • Use soft, even light. Hard light gives shadows, and shadows get read as part of the key.
  • Put the card on a dark surface.
  • Side A: key face up in the recess. Side B: flip it over and photograph again.

Keyway profile

The side photos give the key's outline. The keyway profile is the cross-section of the blade, including its grooves. Choose how to get it:

Slot photo

1 · Print the gauge (once per key size)

The gauge is a small L-shaped stand. The key lies on its base and pokes through a hole in the upright face, which hides the bow so only the tip's outline shows. Measure the blade with calipers so the hole fits snugly.

2 · Take the photo

  1. Lay the key on the gauge's base, side A up, with the cut edge on your right as you face the front of the gauge.
  2. Slide it forward through the hole until 3–5 mm of the tip sticks out in front.
  3. Photograph the front face head-on from about 30 cm, with the phone at about the height of the hole and the whole face in the picture. Soft light from the front lights the key's end best.
  4. Place the four handles on the corners of the front face, with handle 1 at the top-left.
  1. Rest the card across a mug or between two books so the key can hang underneath.
  2. Push the key up through the slot from below, tip first, with the cut edge toward the arrow and the side A face toward the recess. About 8 mm of the tip should stick out.
  3. Pack Prestik or Blu Tack into the slot around the key, level with the card. It holds the key upright and hides the gap.
  4. Hold the phone directly above the slot. Measure roughly how far the tip sticks out and how high the camera is.

  2. Photograph straight on, with the lock face and card filling the frame.

Trace the profile

The app starts from the middle of the hole and spreads out to pixels of a similar colour. If it picked the wrong thing, choose Seed and tap on the key's end. Tidy up with the threshold slider and the lasso. For a quick clean-up, choose Keep inside and tap a box around the key's end.

Profile library

Built in: 35 real keyway profiles (Kwikset, Schlage, Best, Master and Medeco) traced by the open-source keygen project (CC0), plus some generic shapes. These are US keyways. Common South African keyways such as Union and Yale SA aren't published anywhere, so trace yours with a slot or lock-face photo and save them here. The suggestions get better as your library grows.
Faces: which side is A isn't recorded for the built-in profiles, so if the preview looks mirrored, tick "Swap faces A/B" on Build.

Manual grooves

Describe the grooves on each face. Position is measured from the bottom (uncut) edge of the blade to the groove's centre. Auto-detect fills in where the grooves are and how wide they are from your side photos. Depth can't be seen from above, so each groove is given the assumed depth. Adjust the depths using calipers or by comparing with the lock.
